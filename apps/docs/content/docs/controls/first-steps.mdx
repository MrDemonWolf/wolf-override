This quick tutorial covers the basics of your first session without giving away puzzle solutions. You play the engineer; WOLF is an independent companion who moves and acts on his own. The keys below are the defaults; [Input](/docs/controls/input/) lists the controller and touch equivalents.

## 1. Start a new run

Choose **New Game** from the title screen. The opening scene shows WOLF and the Director before you enter the facility. Press **E** (or tap **CONTINUE** on a touch build) to advance each beat. Press **Esc** to pause; choose **Skip Opening** from Pause to go straight to play.

The title screen also has **CREDITS** and **CHANGELOG**, which open inside the game. The changelog screen lists the same dated updates as [this website's changelog](/docs/changelog/); scroll it with the mouse wheel, by dragging the text or with up/down, and use **OPEN FULL CHANGELOG ONLINE** for the website version. **BACK TO TITLE**, Esc, controller B or Android Back return to the title.

## 2. Move and look around

Use **A / D** or **← / →** to move left and right. On a controller, use the left stick or D-pad. On a touch build, hold the on-screen direction buttons.

Watch for labelled stations and the nearby prompt. The prompt tells you when you are close enough to interact and which button your current input setup uses.

At the start, a small tutorial box near the top of the screen shows how to move and suggests reading the nearby display. That display is optional. The objective card points you to your first real goal; once you head that way, the tutorial box follows it and disappears when you arrive or interact with anything.

## 3. Interact with the world

Press the prompted interact button (**E** by default; **USE** on touch) when you are near a station. Interacting can reveal information, advance dialogue, or operate something. Some stations are optional; follow the current objective card and station labels to make progress.

The game briefly shows an objective card when your goal changes. If you are unsure what to do, check the station prompt before trying another control.

A few machines need more than a tap. When the prompt says to **hold** interact (on touch the button reads **CRANK**), keep the button held: each moment held is one real turn of the machine, and turns you have made stay done if you let go. There are no other buttons to learn; everything uses move, interact and the two answers.

## 4. Choose how to respond

When numbered responses appear, choose **1** or **2**, click a response, or use the displayed controller or touch option. Take your time: dialogue and choices wait for your input.

WOLF has his own boundaries. A choice can change what he says or does, but the current chapter keeps essential progress available to the engineer too. You always control the engineer, never WOLF.

## 5. Knockdowns

Later in the chapter the building itself is dangerous: steam, blasts and machines can knock the engineer down. The screen flashes red, the engineer falls (with **REDUCED MOTION** on, the fall is skipped and only the thud, a shorter red wash and the fade play), and after a moment the game returns you to its last automatic save in that room. You lose only the step you were on. The dialogue line names what hit you and how to avoid it, and your earlier choices are kept. There is no penalty for being knocked down or for taking the careful route.

## 6. Pause and save

Press **Esc** during play to pause. Reach the green **SAFE POINT** and interact with it to save a checkpoint; after that, the game also saves automatically at key moments in the later rooms. From the title screen, **Continue** resumes the latest valid save. There is one checkpoint file in the current build.

For exactly how Continue, New Game, and checkpoint recovery work, see [Saving](/docs/controls/saves/). If something misbehaves, check [Settings](/docs/controls/settings/) or [Troubleshooting](/docs/controls/troubleshooting/).
