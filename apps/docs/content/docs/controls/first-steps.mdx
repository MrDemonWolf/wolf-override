This quick tutorial covers the basics of your first session without giving away puzzle solutions. You play the engineer; WOLF is an independent companion who moves and acts on his own. The keys below are the defaults; [Controls](/docs/controls/input/) lists the controller and touch equivalents.

## 1. Start a new run

Choose **New Game** from the title screen. The opening scene shows WOLF and the Director before you enter the facility. Press **E** (or tap **CONTINUE** on a touch build) to show the next line; when two people speak in one beat, each gets their own box. The opening's first line reminds you that **PAUSE** skips it: press **Esc** (or the on-screen **PAUSE**) and choose **Skip Opening** to go straight to play.

The title screen also has **CREDITS** and **CHANGELOG**, which open inside the game. The game's version number sits under **CHANGELOG**. The changelog screen lists the short lines of each version from [this website's changelog](/docs/changelog/) (the full notes stay on the website); scroll it with the mouse wheel, by dragging the text or with up/down, and use **OPEN FULL CHANGELOG ONLINE** for the website version. **BACK TO TITLE**, Esc, controller B or Android Back return to the title.

## 2. Move and look around

Use **A / D** or **← / →** to move left and right. On a controller, use the left stick or D-pad. On a touch build, hold the on-screen direction buttons.

WOLF walks with you on his own. He keeps about a body length away on the side you are not facing, so the way ahead stays clear; when you turn around he crosses behind you to your other side, and next to a wall or a shut door he stays on the open side.

Watch for labelled stations and the nearby prompt. The prompt tells you when you are close enough to interact and which button your current input setup uses.

When you first get control, only two things show: a card with the engineer's name and a small prompt near the top of the screen saying how to move. Your first step fades the name card; the prompt then suggests reading the nearby display, which is optional, and the opening lines begin at the bottom of the screen, one short box at a time. If you stand still, the objective card follows the name card and points you to your first real goal. Once you head right, the tutorial box points on to the breaker and disappears when you arrive or interact with anything; while it shows, it is the only prompt on screen.

## 3. Interact with the world

Press the prompted interact button (**E** by default; **USE** on touch) when you are near a station. Interacting can reveal information, advance dialogue, or operate something. Some stations are optional; follow the current objective card and station labels to make progress.

The game briefly shows an objective card when your goal changes. If you are unsure what to do, check the station prompt before trying another control.

A press that cannot do anything yet still answers: in the Service Junction the machine's lamp flickers, a short dull clunk plays and a line says why. Press again and the dialogue box blinks as the line comes back. If you stay stuck there for a while, WOLF looks at what matters, and later the engineer says more.

A few machines need more than a tap. When the prompt says to **hold** interact (on touch the button reads **CRANK**), keep the button held: the wheel gives as soon as you press, each moment held is one real turn of the machine, and turns you have made stay done if you let go. If holding a button is hard, turn on **TOGGLE HOLDS** in Settings and a single press keeps the crank turning. There are no other buttons to learn; everything uses move, interact and the two answers.

## 4. Choose how to respond

When numbered responses appear, choose **1** or **2**, click a response, or use the displayed controller option. On a touch build the two answers are buttons at the bottom of the screen, within reach of your thumbs, named by their words alone. Take your time: a question stays up until you answer it.

## Reading the dialogue

Lines appear in a box at the bottom of the screen, one speaker at a time, with the speaker's name beside the words (narration has no name). A box holds at most two short rows; a longer line is split at its sentence breaks into boxes that follow one another, without changing a word. Each box stays up for a reading time set by its length (about a third of a second per word or a twentieth of a second per character, whichever is longer, plus a moment), then the next one takes its place, so you can keep walking while people talk. The last box stays until you move on. In the opening and the closing scenes, interact shows the next box at once; at a station where the hint says **continue**, it does the same.

If the text is hard to read, raise **TEXT SIZE** in [Settings](/docs/controls/settings/#display) (100% to 200%). It enlarges the dialogue, the hint line, the cards, the tutorial prompt, the answer buttons and the station names.

WOLF has his own boundaries. A choice can change what he says or does, but the current chapter keeps essential progress available to the engineer too. You always control the engineer, never WOLF.

## 5. Knockdowns

Later in the chapter the building itself is dangerous: steam, blasts and machines can knock the engineer down. The screen flashes red and the engineer drops to their knees and slumps forward onto their side on the floor, the way they were facing (with **REDUCED MOTION** on they are shown lying there at once, with the thud and a shorter red wash), and the dialogue line names what hit you. From just under a second after the hit, the hint line offers **E  RETRY** (**A  RETRY** on a controller; on touch it reads **TAP RETRY** and the **USE** button reads **RETRY**). The screen waits for it and never moves on by itself; pressing it fades to black and back into the room's last automatic save, with control returning about half a second after the press, so about 1.5 seconds after the hit if you press at once. The timing is the same for every knockdown, the first and each repeat. Pause works while the card is up. You lose only the step you were on. The line names how to avoid what hit you, and your earlier choices are kept. There is no penalty for being knocked down or for taking the careful route.

## 6. Pause and save

Press **Esc** during play to pause. Reach the green **SAFE POINT** and interact with it to save a checkpoint; after that, the game also saves automatically at key moments in the later rooms. From the title screen, **Continue** resumes the latest valid save. There is one checkpoint file in the current build.

For exactly how Continue, New Game, and checkpoint recovery work, see [Saving](/docs/controls/saves/). If something misbehaves, check [Settings](/docs/controls/settings/) or [Troubleshooting](/docs/controls/troubleshooting/). The optional [chapter walkthrough](/docs/walkthrough/) is marked for spoilers.
