## The idea

WOLF//OVERRIDE starts with an autonomous robotic wolf who wakes himself and refuses a plan to use him as a weapon. The player is the engineer who meets him. The central design question is how two characters with their own agency can work together without turning WOLF into a tool the player controls.

Nathanial Henniges created the original concept, core story, and creative direction. Research has informed craft and plausibility; it did not originate the game or story. The [credits and references](/docs/credits/) explain those sources and the tools used.

## Build a small playable chapter

The first public source slice established the engineer as the sole playable character, with WOLF acting independently. A maintenance corridor gives the pair a shared problem: restore power and open a sealed door. WOLF can cooperate or refuse, while the engineer always has a manual way to continue. This puts his boundaries into play without blocking progress.

The chapter then moves into Records Access, where the engineer preserves a purge-order trace. A choice from the corridor is remembered later, and a safe point lets the player continue from saved chapter progress. Those rooms make a small, testable foundation for the larger story direction.

## Add the systems around play

The prototype grew to include a player-paced opening, location and objective prompts, controller and touch input, remappable controls, display and audio settings, and checkpoint recovery. These systems help players understand the current slice while keeping the engineer in control of essential actions.

The site and source documentation grew alongside the game: setup instructions, controls, room-by-room puzzle help, current status, testing boundaries, credits, and a public changelog. The [changelog](/docs/changelog/) lists dated implementation updates; the [testing page](/docs/development/testing/) distinguishes automated checks from hands-on and device testing.

## Where it stands

The project is still an early source prototype, not a complete campaign. Final character animation, sound, the wider story, public export packages, and complete device playtests remain future work. The website's [Get the game](/docs/get-game/) page explains how to try the source build today and what a packaged release would mean.

Future development should expand the relationship and investigation while preserving WOLF's independence, clear player feedback, and an engineer-only route through essential progress. Proposed features remain plans until they are implemented and checked.
