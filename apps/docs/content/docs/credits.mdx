## Original work

**Original game concept, core story, and creative vision:** Nathanial Henniges. WOLF's self-awakening, his independent bond with the engineer, and the evidence and accountability direction come from Nathanial's concept. **Development:** MrDemonWolf, Inc., with AI assistance directed and reviewed by Nathanial. Research references below inform craft and plausibility; they did not originate the game or its story.

## Research references

- [Fumito Ueda's interview about *The Last Guardian*](https://blog.playstation.com/2016/06/21/the-last-guardian-5-storytelling-secrets/) informed discussion of companion cooperation, quiet moments, and showing a relationship through play.
- [NIST's AI Risk Management Framework Core](https://airc.nist.gov/airmf-resources/airmf/5-sec-core/) informed research on human oversight and accountability.
- [The National Archives' electronic records requirements](https://www.archives.gov/records-mgmt/policy/universalermrequirements) informed research on records, metadata, and preservation.

### UI and accessibility references reviewed

- [Naughty Dog's *The Last of Us Part II* accessibility overview](https://www.naughtydog.com/blog/the_last_of_us_part_ii_accessibility_features_detailed) was reviewed for control remapping, separate audio volumes, speaker labels, and readable dialogue backgrounds. WOLF//OVERRIDE currently has remapping, audio bus controls, and speaker labels. Adjustable dialogue size and background options remain proposed improvements.
- [Game Accessibility Guidelines: player-paced text](https://gameaccessibilityguidelines.com/allow-players-to-progress-through-text-prompts-at-their-own-pace/) was reviewed for dialogue, tutorial, and cinematic pacing. The guide recommends player-controlled advancement, or pause/replay when timed presentation is necessary.
- [Game Accessibility Guidelines: recognizable interactions](https://gameaccessibilityguidelines.com/give-a-clear-indication-that-interactive-elements-are-interactive/) was reviewed for consistent station prompts, contrast, and state feedback. Labels and shapes should support color cues so a player's next action stays clear.
- [Godot 4.7 Camera2D reference](https://docs.godotengine.org/en/4.7/classes/class_camera2d.html) informed the closer horizontal follow camera, its smoothing and room-edge limits.
- [Nielsen Norman Group: reading on mobile](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/mobile-content/) informed the full-size screenshot links when source-game labels became too small in the narrow website view.

These are craft references, not project contributors, sources of copied artwork or story, or endorsements of WOLF//OVERRIDE. Reviewed references do not imply that every feature they describe has been implemented.

### Marketing website review

- [Stray / Annapurna Interactive](https://annapurnainteractive.com/games/stray) was consulted for a concise player-facing premise and clear platform destinations; [Planet of Lana / Wishfully](https://www.planetoflana.com/) for companion-focused imagery; and [REPLACED / Thunderful](https://thunderfulgames.com/games/replaced/) for game media and availability structure. These informed website presentation only. No artwork, wording or story was borrowed; Nathanial retains the original creative vision.
- [Nielsen Norman Group: usability heuristics](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/ten-usability-heuristics/) informed the website review of clear release status and a plain list of planned platforms. An earlier browser-based platform suggestion and picker were removed on October 8, 2026 because no platform has a package to pick.
- [Nielsen Norman Group: how users read on the web](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/how-users-read-on-the-web/) informed the guide's short start page, task-focused navigation, and separation of optional puzzle help from setup and controls.
- [W3C: content on hover or focus](https://www.w3.org/WAI/WCAG22/Understanding/content-on-hover-or-focus.html) informed the download availability treatment: availability is stated in plain text ("no release date" beside each planned platform) with no hover-only hints or tooltips. This review is not a complete accessibility certification.

### Research and references: how the game should play

In October 2026 a research pass studied how a game like WOLF//OVERRIDE should play: pacing, controls, teaching, puzzles, believable spaces, the companion, failure and retry, story told through play, playtesting, phones, sound and readable images. Among about 400 sources, it relied most on the work of these people, studios and organizations:

- **Valve:** the developer commentaries of *Half-Life 2*, its two episodes, *Portal* and *Portal 2*; Mike Ambinder's playtesting talk (GDC 2009); Michael Booth on the AI of *Left 4 Dead* (AIIDE 2009); Ken Birdwell's "The Cabal" (Game Developer, 1999).
- **Playdead:** Jeppe Carlsen on *LIMBO* (GDC Europe, reported by Game Developer); Martin Stig Andersen and Jakob Schmid on the audio of *INSIDE*; Mikkel Svendsen's "Turn It Down 90%" on its effects; Andreas N. Grøntved and Martin Fasterholdt on its animation.
- **Naughty Dog:** Max Dyckhoff, "Ellie: Buddy AI in *The Last of Us*" (*Game AI Pro 2*, 2015); Matthew Gallant on the accessibility of *The Last of Us Part II* (PlayStation Blog, 2020); David Shaver on player navigation (80 Level, 2018).
- **Sony Santa Monica Studio:** Hayato Yoshidome, "Raising Atreus for Battle in *God of War*" (GDC 2019).
- **Nintendo:** Koichi Hayashida on Mario level design (Game Developer, 2012) and *Iwata Asks*.
- **Designers and writers:** Eric Chahi's *Another World* postmortem (GDC 2011); Kim Swift and Erik Wolpaw on *Portal*; Josef Fares on *Brothers: A Tale of Two Sons* (Game Informer, 2013); Steve Swink, *Game Feel*; Mark Brown, Game Maker's Toolkit; Ron Gilbert, "Why Adventure Games Suck"; Liz England, "The Door Problem"; Jenova Chen, "Flow in Games"; Mick West, "Programming Responsiveness"; Noel Berry and Maddy Thorson's released *Celeste* player code.
- **Games user research:** Steve Bromley; Erik Andersen and colleagues' study of tutorials (CHI 2012); Heather Desurvire and Bernard Chen's playability principles; Jakob Nielsen on response times.
- **Platform and accessibility guidelines:** Apple's Human Interface Guidelines, Microsoft's Xbox Accessibility Guidelines, Google's Android app quality guidelines, Valve's Steam Deck compatibility criteria, the Game Accessibility Guidelines, AbleGamers' Accessible Player Experiences, the BBC Subtitle Guidelines and W3C's WCAG 2.2.
- **Godot Engine:** its contributors' documentation and source.

These informed craft only. They are not project contributors, no artwork, text or story was taken from them, and none of them endorses WOLF//OVERRIDE. The [full source list](https://github.com/MrDemonWolf/wolf-override/blob/main/docs/research-2026-10/how-it-should-play/SOURCES.md) gives every creator, title, venue, year and link.

## Tools used in the current source build

| Work | Tools |
| --- | --- |
| Game | Godot 4.7.2, typed GDScript, built-in Godot nodes |
| Research, writing, and code assistance | ChatGPT and Codex (OpenAI), and Claude Code by Anthropic as an AI coding assistant, with human review; no runtime AI service |
| Provisional art | ChatGPT Create in the private MDW WOLF//OVERRIDE [Game] project, and the Codex built-in image generation tool (edit mode) for the layered rooms, station props, the Service Junction pressure bulkhead, the paint on the corridor's door end, redrawn character sprites and the current title image; the logo SVG was redrawn from a generated concept |
| Sound effects | Mostly generated by the game's own code from fixed recipes (`scripts/sfx_bank.gd`); the steam heard at the corridor's **MANUAL BYPASS** and in the Service Junction, and two of WOLF's growls, are CC0 recordings processed with project scripts (see [sound recordings](#sound-recordings)). The newer recipes and the recording processing came from AI-assisted sound passes. Nathanial Henniges picked the earlier sounds by ear; the station chirp, WOLF's joint whir and the Service Junction's fuse tick, added on October 13, 2026 in AI-assisted passes, have not been picked by ear yet |
| Public site | Next.js, Fumadocs, MDX, Tailwind CSS 4, TypeScript, and Bun |
| Image processing | Pillow (crops of the junction tank, the near room layers and the engineer's collapse frames, the character downscales), NumPy with Pillow (lining up the pressure bulkhead images, cleaning their edges and downscaling them), NumPy, SciPy and Pillow (the corridor door end's construction drawings, the cleanup of its paint and its atlas), ImageMagick (canvas extension of an early sprite), and cwebp (libwebp) and Chrome's encoder through Playwright for the website's WebP images; screenshots are scripted captures from Godot |
| Checks and hosting | GitHub Actions and GitHub Pages |
| Typefaces | Roboto (The Roboto Project Authors) and Montserrat (The Montserrat Project Authors), both under the SIL Open Font License 1.1 |

The provisional WOLF, engineer, and Director sprites and the first title image were generated with ChatGPT Create. In October 2026 the Codex built-in image generation tool redrew WOLF's head in that title image to remove an extra ear, redrew the three sprites and, using the earlier ChatGPT Create room paintings and machine sheets as references, produced the corridor and Records Access as layered gameplay rooms (far, middle and near plates) with eight station props; using those approved sets as style references, it then produced the Service Junction's layered room, its station machines and the sentry, and painted the current title image as an edit of the first one, with the redrawn sprites and the corridor layers as references. It also produced the pressure bulkhead that stands across the walkway at the Service Junction door (sealed, breaking in the blast and blown open, with the front pillar of its doorway), and a corridor bulkhead standing open on a painted service passage, a cut of which fills the Service Junction's open exit hatch. The corridor's door end (the concrete wall across the corridor, its rolling steel door, the wall-mounted manual bypass, the machinery room beyond and the plinths under the relay and the safe point) was made design first in October 2026: its shapes were drawn by Python scripts from the measured sizes of real doors, gearboxes, conduit and cabinets, the Codex built-in image generation tool painted material and light only inside those shapes, and scripts cleaned up the result; its stencilled marks are typeset in Roboto. Its research, design notes, review rounds and the raw generated images are kept in the repository under `docs/research-2026-10/sources/corridor-door-end-2026-10-12/`. The engineer's collapse when knocked down (five frames: knees, slump, lying on the side) was painted by the same tool over drawn pose guides, with the redrawn sprites and character sheets as references. Nathanial Henniges reviewed and approved each set for this prototype. Each file's source and pending rights review are recorded in the [asset register](https://github.com/MrDemonWolf/wolf-override/blob/main/apps/game/assets/manifest.json). Most sound effects are generated by project code; a few are processed CC0 recordings, credited below. No music or voice is included in the current source build.

## Sound recordings

The steam in the corridor and the Service Junction and two of WOLF's growls start from recordings released under [CC0](https://creativecommons.org/publicdomain/zero/1.0/), processed for the game: cut, looped, filtered and, for the growls, run through a machine-like body. CC0 asks for no credit; these authors are credited with thanks.

- **Joseph Sardin, [BigSoundBank.com](https://bigsoundbank.com/) / LaSonotheque.org**: [Pressure cooker](https://bigsoundbank.com/pressure-cooker-s0805.html), [Air leak](https://bigsoundbank.com/air-leak-s1486.html) and [Air](https://bigsoundbank.com/air-s0138.html), for the hiss of the pressure line and of the corridor's **MANUAL BYPASS**, the relief vent's burst and the coolant cloud.
- **pauliuw**: [Dog sounds](https://opengameart.org/content/dog-sounds) on OpenGameArt.org, for WOLF's growl at the door seam and the voice in his refusal growl.
- **bonebrah**: [Dog Growl](https://opengameart.org/content/dog-growl) on OpenGameArt.org, the rasp in WOLF's refusal growl.
- **AntumDeluge**: [Bear Growls](https://opengameart.org/content/bear-growls) on OpenGameArt.org, recorded by the U.S. Fish & Wildlife Service, a faint texture in WOLF's refusal growl.

Each file's source, licence and processing are recorded in the [asset register](https://github.com/MrDemonWolf/wolf-override/blob/main/apps/game/assets/manifest.json).
