## Adopted direction

October 30 follow-up: a security escort record first identifies the investigator as the person moved on the freight lift (Nathanial chose A). This does not alone establish the label was false. Chapter 1 ends after the engineer discovers their repair enabled the transfer; the next goal is to find the investigator. Nathanial authorized implementation, agent audits and end-to-end checks. Exact record, access and dialogue are implementation drafts, not separately approved wording. Destination, character fates, escalation and campaign endings remain open.

W.O.L.F. is an AI made for good, to help bring the world together; he was never meant to kill workers or start a war, and that origin never makes him property (ST-01). He already occupies his robotic wolf body. In the opening he hears the Director order him to kill a witness, a records worker who preserved proof of the Director's wrongdoing. Nobody activates him: WOLF wakes himself, refuses and escapes containment inside THE DEN, where he meets the systems-engineer protagonist (ST-04). He has killed no one. The engineer once did an ordinary maintenance job that, unknown to them, helped the Director; they discover that later in the investigation, with WOLF, never at the opening (ST-03).

The Program Director is the human antagonist. He is deliberately evil and protects his personal power: he falsely labelled an investigator dangerous and had them secretly detained to silence exposure (ST-02). After the lockdown, the engineer and WOLF discover the missing investigator and the engineer's unwitting role, search for the investigator and try to protect them, and preserve corroborating proof beyond the Director's reach. The spine is wrongdoing, corroborating evidence, discovery, consequence (ST-05). The investigator's fate is open. Nathanial authorized the later Chapter 1 discoveries in their authored gameplay scene on October 30; keep them out of the opening, website, README and unrelated build output.

WOLF is an independent companion, potentially a friend and chosen family. He can enjoy pet-like affection while having intelligence, boundaries, choices and responsibility. Belonging WITH someone is not belonging TO someone. Mutual care matters; closeness and restraint are not interchangeable.

The systems engineer is the sole playable viewpoint for the whole game. WOLF is visible, speaks and acts as an independent authored companion; the player can ask, cooperate and disagree but never takes control of him. His refusal cannot stop essential progress. Keep his self-awakening and later independent choices legible from the engineer's viewpoint without a playable WOLF prologue.

The protagonist and supporting humans should have natural personal names. Engineer remains an occupation or internal role. Name/pronouns remain independent of a small cosmetic appearance roster; no full creator or identity-linked morality.

The current playable engineer is non-binary (assigned male at birth), masculine-presenting, and uses they/them pronouns with every provisional display name (Nathanial, October 12, 2026). The selected provisional sprite wears a coherent deep-red hooded field jacket with charcoal trousers and boots; the earlier yellow patches and silver knee armor were rejected. This visual does not establish military rank, final character art or a final name.

The sunrise payoff: the protagonist pets WOLF at sunrise after they leave THE DEN together. Affection is the payoff of the earned relationship, not a timed test or point-farming mechanic. The game has multiple endings, 2 or 3, and Nathanial leans toward two different endings to keep it simple (ST-08); the final count and which endings are open. World stakes come only through Nathanial's proposed, unlocked idea that the Director later escalates to wanting to nuke the world to cover up his crimes (ST-10), never as WOLF's collective punishment.

## Stable production direction
Godot and typed GDScript; 2D side-view gameplay with cinematic 2.5D presentation, layers/parallax/lighting. Grounded military sci-fi and dark painterly horror. Tone moves from funny/strange through unease to serious horror. SITE 51 and connected underground THE DEN. Eventual campaign target 3–5 hours; Mac first with Windows/iPhone/iPad/Android validation early.

The player controls the engineer for repairs, tools/terminals, hiding, puzzles and conversations. WOLF tracks, traverses, hacks and protects through authored companion actions he chooses; any later combat remains moderate. There is no character switching. Light platforming, light-to-medium puzzles, story upgrades and frequent checkpoints remain the direction. Increasingly demonic appearance never implies evil.

Authored dialogue and deterministic memory rules; no runtime LLM. No visible morality meter. WOLF can refuse story requests; both characters can lie. Evidence, NPCs or confession reveal lies. Quote only real observed or later-discovered choices. Basic controls always remain reliable; required objectives have alternatives. Self-defense, fear, assertiveness, identity, accessibility, one rude reply, one missed reaction or optional clue cannot lock a bad outcome. Later meaningful actions can repair trust before clear commitments. WOLF remains responsible for his own harm.

Humans use written dialogue. Only selected WOLF lines use optional synthetic/demonic TTS with readable subtitles, mute and text fallback. Separate spoken text and delivery notes. Verify actual voices/offline behavior later. Nicknames come from a finite authored set without unwanted abbreviations or inferred gender.

The engineer copies a purge-order trace and mirror chronology in Records Access; those records do not establish the later crime. WOLF can help or decline, and engineer-only routes remain essential. The approved opening order and existing Look 2 artwork stay unchanged.

The October 30 candidate extends the Service Junction exit into a maintenance annex using existing registered Records room art as provisional staging. Three engineer-operated stations recover a surviving transfer fragment, compare it with controller-origin movement, then read a security escort record and crosscheck the engineer's repair. Each step has its own source, limit, visible response and next action. A second copy of one record is not independent corroboration. A matching trip alone does not prove who was aboard; an escort record identifies the investigator but does not by itself prove the dangerous label was false.

The engineer knows their own maintenance history before the reveal. The discovery is what security used their repair for, not a suddenly remembered job. WOLF may react, look, wait or choose to help without becoming a player-controlled tool. No essential station depends on him. Chapter 1 ends with the repair connection understood and the next goal to find the investigator.
