# DESIGN REVIEW

## 1. Contradictions or gaps between these plans

1. **A safe point currently means several different things.** `design-reference.md` targets safe points at doorways and says they should protect evidence; `death-safe-points.json` makes one a hazard-free, held-USE ritual, case backup, disk save, and room retry point. `level-expansion.json` says “no disk save at any door,” moves Junction autosave A to S1, and drops the Records safe-point prop because a save follows nearby. Lock the terms: door entry = restore state; SAFE POINT = always hazard-free refuge, evidence secure, and local retry. Keep or remove a station based on the refuge/WOLF beat, not only save spacing.

2. **The CASE capacity does not agree.** `design-reference.md` proposes at most about 6 carried slots including key items, plus one tool. `level-expansion.json` calls the case “approved” at 8 items and lists pickups across all three rooms. Confirm whether that means 7 case entries plus the tool or 8 entries plus the tool; either way, publish one cap and show the crowded-case behavior before adding more pickups.

3. **The art schedule and available generator disagree.** `ART_BIBLE.md` puts a style test before art batches and says room plates wait for playable blockouts, but `level-expansion.json` puts title/hero art first and expects 2560×1440 title and mid plates. The bible’s CLI route requires an API key; the built-in tool tops out around 1672×941 in the measured examples. With built-in generation only, size the title and plates around that limit or compose/scale them deliberately; do not promise native 2560×1440 detail.

4. **WOLF’s future command and off-screen rules are not frozen.** `design-reference.md` places a possible player “request” verb in Later and describes off-camera scouting by sound and posture. The current decisions are no player request verb yet, and off-screen WOLF must use sound plus a small edge marker. Put those in the shared interaction rules so neither the route graph nor tutorials quietly introduce a command or a full-screen locator.

5. **Door art still has an open A/B question.** `ART_BIBLE.md` asks whether to retain the A/B redraws, while the chosen direction is door A: a bulkhead across the walkway. `level-expansion.json` specifies a 64 px opening with a separate 132 px leaf, but does not name that as A. Carry the selected blocking silhouette through the access door, lift, and pressure door families, then differentiate those families with state and framing.

6. **The engineer-only guarantee is stated but not demonstrated room by room.** The corridor describes a WOLF-cooperate / engineer-fallback relay, while the level plan also introduces WOLF-only perches and route gates. Add an objective matrix for every essential flag and item: engineer route, WOLF assist, refusal behavior, and recovery after death. “Room works if WOLF never moves” needs to cover the actual objective state, not only traversal.

7. **Death pacing has incompatible targets.** `design-reference.md` sets roughly 20 seconds of replay per death and at most 1.45 seconds to control, with a skippable flourish. `death-safe-points.json` allows a 4–5 second first-death sequence, 2 seconds on repeats, and about 30 seconds of replay. Pick one timing contract. Keep the death card readable and player-controlled, but allow a skip that gets the engineer back to control within 1.45 seconds; separately time the whole retry route and keep it under 20 seconds.

8. **“Screens” use different widths.** `design-reference.md` calculates about 711 world px in view at zoom 1.35; `level-expansion.json` calls the 1680 px corridor 1.75 screens, which divides by the 960 px room canvas instead. At gameplay zoom, that corridor is about 2.36 view widths. Check camera beats and phone framing against 711 px, not the canvas width.

## 2. The 10 most important refinements

1. **Build a playable slice before the title art.** Lock the route/state checklist, then greybox the 1680 px corridor from handover through relay choice, door A, SAFE POINT, and Records exit. Test at zoom 1.35 with three people who have not seen the plan; target 1–3 minutes for the room and no blocked progress. Only then freeze its RoomProfile, build Records, then the Junction, and paint the approved route. The proposed 31-day plan spends 1.5 days on title/marketing art before the first playable greybox; that order risks polishing an unproven route. *(Evidence: `level-expansion.json` plan phases and corridor; `design-reference.md` room targets; Inside’s authored pacing.)*

2. **Prove the engineer-only path for every essential state.** Make a four-column matrix for relay/breaker, seal, each essential Records trace, Junction valve, and blast door: engineer alone; WOLF helps; WOLF refuses; death/re-entry. Mark each required flag as reachable in the first column. WOLF-only perches and shortcuts can stay optional, but no essential flag, key item, or exit may require him to move. *(Evidence: `level-expansion.json` corridor relay fallback and WOLF route graph; `real-inventory-design.json` key rules; Stray and Brothers/Plague Tale lessons in `design-reference.md`.)*

3. **Make WOLF’s choices legible without turning them into commands.** Keep the intent-vote behaviors, but acknowledge a player input or scene trigger within 150 ms and show the choice in posture/sound within 0.8 s; if he refuses, he still clears the engineer route. With no request verb, teach his interest through sniff/listen/look-before-lead. When he is off-screen, use one quiet sound plus a 24–32 px edge pip; remove the pip within 150 ms of his return. Never show a companion locator while he is on-screen. *(Evidence: `design-reference.md` says missed input can look like disobedience and gives a 711 px view; the chosen off-screen rule; Trico and Planet of Lana lessons.)*

4. **Give safe points one dependable promise and unify death timing.** A SAFE POINT is always safe: no lethal or knockdown trigger can activate there, and no enemy state can force the engineer into danger while using it. One cancellable 1.0–1.2 s hold secures the case and sets the local retry. A death may drop one visible, one-tap-recoverable bundle outside the killing trigger; solved states and essential evidence never enter it. Make the death card skippable to control within 1.45 s and keep a full retry under 20 s; leave the card up until the player advances instead of forcing a longer sequence. *(Evidence: `death-safe-points.json` recommends a hazard-free ritual and recoverable bundle; `design-reference.md` sets ≤1.45 s control, ≲20 s replay, and zero essential evidence loss; Celeste and Hollow Knight.)*

5. **Set the CASE cap before more pickups land.** Choose six fixed slots including keys, plus the one tool; allow at most two unsecured essentials between SAFE POINTs. Put records in the pause-only CASE and preserve their solved facts outside the death-loss bundle. No grid, sorting, or long examine holds. This resolves the “about six” target against the level plan’s eight-item case list before UI and save migration expand around it. *(Evidence: `real-inventory-design.json`; `design-reference.md` CASE targets; Signalis and Resident Evil.)*

6. **Pace to the actual camera, not the room canvas.** At 190 px/s, the 1600 px corridor walk line is about 8.4 seconds end to end; at zoom 1.35 the visible width is about 711 px, so it spans 2.36 views. Keep the opening tutorial’s short 1.6 s gaps, then allow quiet stretches but no more than 3.7 s of empty walking before a meaningful beat. Keep each new room at 1–3 minutes and use no fail-able jumps or shallow-Z hazards. *(Evidence: `design-reference.md` current values and targets; `level-expansion.json` room widths and 1.6 s spacing; Inside, Little Nightmares, and Planet of Lana.)*

7. **Treat mobile controls as part of the blockout.** Prototype an anchored, visible movement pad and a 56 dp context USE button; keep Pause at 44 dp or larger. Show the two relay choices only during that decision. The same USE control should support tap and a visible cancellable hold; do not require swipe-to-interact or timing QTEs. Measure thumb occlusion and missed taps on a small phone before painting the plate; the touch-target sizes are explicitly missing from the research. *(Evidence: `design-reference.md` mobile notes; `ART_BIBLE.md` minimum 44 world px for interactive props; Inside iOS and Planet of Lana mobile fixes.)*

8. **Add feedback in the smallest useful set.** Keep the current 190 px/s movement and prototype only a short start/stop anticipation layer before changing physics. Add three footstep bands—dry plate, wet plate, grating—and one room-tone bed per room; use the existing state-driven prop glows and pressure hiss before adding new systems. The current inventory reports no footsteps, room tone, or ambient one-shots, so these give useful weight and threat location without a large sound list. *(Evidence: `real-inventory-design.json`; `design-reference.md` audio targets and Inside’s breathing/audio-system lessons.)*

9. **Spend full impact effects only on three major beats.** Reserve the full shake/hit-stop/flash/rumble stack for the door blast, a sentry catch, and one major story reveal. Use sound and a state change for ordinary USEs. Keep the existing reduced-motion and effects switches wired through every effect; make state lamps and the selected bulkhead silhouette carry door readability. Preserve the bible’s 44-world-px minimum for interactive props and 2-screen-px actor details. *(Evidence: `real-inventory-design.json` impact kit and switches; `ART_BIBLE.md` size/readability rules; the binding impact and door-A decisions.)*

10. **Make the art plan fit built-in generation and the playable route.** Approve one style test first. Use built-in image generation for the title and plates too, compose title/menu text in-engine, and accept the measured ~1672×941 source rather than promising native 2560×1440. Validate the first plate and door at 960×540, 1280×720, and 1920×1080 before batching. Keep website hero, share card, and README derivatives behind in-game approval so gameplay changes do not force a second marketing-art pass. *(Evidence: `ART_BIBLE.md` measured built-in output and style-test gate; `level-expansion.json` 2560×1440 CLI assumption and P0 scope.)*

## 3. Risks to fun, readability, scope and mobile

- **Fun:** Spacing a new station or reveal every 1.6 seconds can make the corridor feel like a tutorial checklist instead of a quiet horror scene. The planned recoverable CASE drop adds useful stakes, but losing a second drop can make optional records feel like chores; keep all required facts and exits independent of carried evidence, and let SAFE POINTs secure it often. WOLF’s refusal only works if the engineer’s alternate path is obvious and immediate, not if refusal becomes a hidden puzzle state. *(Evidence: `level-expansion.json` corridor beat spacing; `death-safe-points.json` second-death loss; `design-reference.md` says refusals should be rare and clearly telegraphed.)*

- **Readability:** The planned 1680 px corridor is over two actual camera views at zoom 1.35. A wide view with a tower landmark, reflections, foreground bulkhead, the WOLF route above the engineer, lamps, and no persistent HUD can obscure which surfaces are climbable or interactive. Keep the engineer on one plane, WOLF-only surfaces at least 24 px above walk lines, and test the bulkhead and every station at 960×540 before increasing background detail. *(Evidence: `design-reference.md` camera/height rules and visible width; `level-expansion.json` WOLF heights and room composition; `ART_BIBLE.md` detail budget.)*

- **Scope:** The plan combines three expanded rooms, new room/profile and camera systems, route graphs, saved CASE state, a death/drop model, art for several families, and title/website/share/README outputs in about 31 working days. The plan itself says none of the new rooms has been built or played. Each new shared system multiplies failure cases across saves, room transitions, death, and WOLF refusal; treat the day count as a rough estimate and make each room pass its blockout gate before starting the next. *(Evidence: `level-expansion.json` summary, shared-tech list, and P0–P phases; `ART_BIBLE.md` asset QA gates.)*

- **Mobile:** Touch target sizes are unresearched, and the 44 px art rule is in world units, not a tested phone touch target. A visible movement pad plus USE, Pause, and temporary choice buttons can cover the engineer, WOLF, or the next hazard; a long hold can also tire a thumb. Check thumb reach, occlusion, text legibility, and opposite-direction input on a small phone, then profile a 20-minute session on a cool device. *(Evidence: `design-reference.md` explicitly lists touch sizing and session length as gaps and warns about invisible controls/holds/thermal limits; `ART_BIBLE.md` sizes props, not touch zones.)*

## 4. What I would cut

- **Cut title/website/share-card production from the first gameplay gate.** Keep the approved title art B as a mood reference; do not replace the title, hero, share card, or README until the corridor route is playable and approved. This removes the plan’s highest-polish, lowest-learning task from P0.

- **Cut the CLI/API-key art route entirely.** It is incompatible with the built-in-only decision. Generate the needed image families with the built-in tool and make composition/text do the work; do not spend time defending 2560×1440 output that the available generator cannot supply.

- **Cut cross-room death recovery and extra save formats.** Keep one room-local retry point plus the chosen recoverable drop. Defer one-shot suspend files, cross-room respawns, re-entry dialogue state, and death-based shortcuts through the blast door; the judged drafts already flag those as migration and soft-lock scope.

- **Cut the over-cap pickup batch and deduction UI until the CASE limit is settled.** Ship the six-slot pause-only CASE first; defer extra record items, grids, sorting, and multi-clue deduction validation. Keep optional records as authored discoveries, and keep their underlying essential facts safe.

- **Cut new WOLF request verbs, trust meters, and persistent locator HUD.** Preserve his autonomous choice and refusal through behavior, sound, and the small off-screen edge pip. A request can be reconsidered after the base companion loop proves readable.

- **Cut generalized room/backtracking machinery until a second real use requires it.** Keep the planned one-way doors, simple RoomProfile data, and existing save paths; defer bidirectional door routing, chapter/room identity splitting, and generic return-room dialogue state.

- **Cut full impact feedback from routine actions.** Use the full effect stack only for the few large moments; ordinary switches, pickups, and SAFE POINTs need clear state audio/light, not camera shake and hit-stop.

## 5. Questions for Nathanial

1. **What does a SAFE POINT commit?** Recommended: it secures the CASE, writes a disk save, and becomes the room-local death retry; door entry itself remains a transition/restore state, not a save.

2. **What happens to optional evidence after a second death before recovery?** Keep the judged model’s one-recovery rule and permanent loss for that save, or return optional items to their original spots? Essential facts remain protected either way.

3. **Which CASE cap should the level plan use?** Recommended: six fixed slots including key items, plus the separate tool. Should the plan reduce its eight-item list to fit, or are some of those items secured outside the CASE?

4. **How often should WOLF refuse in the first chapter?** Recommended: one clearly telegraphed refusal with an engineer-only fallback, then additional refusals only where they create a new story beat.

5. **Is mobile a first-slice target or a later port?** If it is first-slice, the route, camera, thumb zones, and text need to pass on a small phone before the room plates are approved.

6. **Which scene should the title image depict after the route is approved?** The existing approved key art B, or the new containment bay/relay tower from the level plan? I recommend deferring the choice until the greybox proves the landmark composition.
