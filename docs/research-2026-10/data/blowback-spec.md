# Blowback: Service Junction — implementation spec (user-approved 2026-10-08)

## Decisions
- Disabling a machine environmentally is acceptable (non-lethal toward people).
- The relay memory keeps gating whether WOLF helps; the engineer path always exists.
- Audio: procedural SFX first (generated at startup); CC0 swaps later via the asset register.
- Zero new input actions: hold-to-move, USE (tap/hold), choice 1/2, pause.

## Pitch
A third 960-px room right after the Records exit. The service line to the Archive is sealed and something hums behind the door. The engineer has no weapon, only the building: overload the coolant line and crank a valve to blow the sealed door, drop a hanging coolant tank on the purge sentry the blast woke up, then dump the overload into the exit bolt. Two big booms and one arc in about three minutes, each with a timing window and a blast zone. WOLF scouts, reads and, if he chooses, baits the sentry under the tank; every tool is engineer-operated. A knockdown reloads the current beat's autosave, never more than ~20 s lost.

## Where
New `JunctionRoom` node in scenes/main.tscn shown when state.chapter_id == "junction" (records_room.gd pattern), entered by USE at the Records exit (x 830) after complete_chapter(), replacing today's 'Archive trail is next' card. Same floor (y 440), same 40..920 clamp, same camera clamp. Left to right: entry hatch x 80; OVERLOAD BREAKER x 200; relief vent grate x 300 (250..350 is its blast zone); VALVE WHEEL x 420; SEALED DOOR x 480 (StaticBody2D 56x132 on layer 2, same as the corridor door; blast zone 360..540); rubble line x 540; ARM PANEL alcove 556..584 (counts as cover); sentry lane 600..840; hanging tank at x 700 (rest y 250, floor mark drawn at 700); EXIT BOLT PANEL x 850; exit hatch x 880.

## WOLF role
Scouts, reads and holds; never operates a tool. Beat 0 he reads the pressure at the door. Beat 2 he refuses to cross the rubble while the sentry is live, marking the lane as dangerous without text. Beat 3/4, if asked and the relay memory is DISCLOSE (the same rule activate_power uses today), he chooses to bait the sentry under the arm; with PRESS memory he visibly refuses; after a missed drop he breaks off for that attempt. Beat 5 he positions himself between the engineer and the dark. Whether the memory gates his help or only colours his line is an open question for the user.

## Engineer fallback
Breaker, valve, arm panel and bolt panel are all engineer stations. The tank drop is timeable on the patrol alone: sentry at 140 px/s through an 88 px hit window gives ~0.6 s of valid tap per pass, with the drawn floor mark and the 6 s rewind for an in-place retry. If WOLF refuses or breaks off, the status line says so and points at the mark, like the relay bypass today.

## Fail and retry
One path, _fail_beat(reason): knockdown sources are the relief vent blowing with the engineer on it, the door blast zone at fuse end, sentry contact, and the coolant cloud. Sequence: hit-stop 60 ms, red flash, human sprite tilts ~70 degrees and slides 30 px with a thud and strong rumble, controls off 1.1 s, IntroFade to black 0.4 s, then _load_game() from the single slot, which is the autosave at the start of the current beat (A entry, B door blown, C sentry down). _sync_scene resets transient state: PressureLine idle, breaker unarmed, tank re-hung, sentry at 840 in patrol, wolf_baiting false, Engine.time_scale 1.0, camera offset zero. Status line names the cause and the fix. Loss is bounded to the current beat. No fail counter; memory and Records flags untouched.

## Input
Zero new actions. Keyboard: A/D move, E USE (tap to arm, drop, route; HOLD at the valve), 1/2 choice, Esc pause. Controller: stick/D-pad, A USE (hold to crank), X/Y choice, Start pause, B cancel; rumble gated on controller_active. Touch: hold-to-move, USE button (hold at the valve; label relabels to CRANK / DROP like the existing CONTINUE relabel), Choice 1/2 replace the row during the choice; vibrate_handheld gated on touch_enabled. _pause_game's release list is unchanged; the sentry brain and PressureLine do not tick while paused or during a choice.

## Audio
Procedural only, via the shared SfxBank (AudioStreamWAV buffers generated at startup, 16-bit mono 22.05 kHz, peak -6 dB), all on the existing 'Effects' bus after GameSettings.configure() so the slider and mute apply on day one. Recipes: BOOM 0.9 s noise burst through a low-pass sweeping 2 kHz -> 150 Hz plus a 55 -> 30 Hz sub, soft-clipped; SMALL BOOM 0.4 s without the sub; HISS looping filtered noise with volume_db = lerp(-28, -8, gauge); PUMP HUM 48 Hz saw with pitch_scale 1 + 0.6 * gauge; CLICK/CLANK 30 ms noise tick with a 400 Hz ring; KLAXON 3 x 120 ms square chirps; WINCH 6 s 90 Hz saw with 7 Hz wobble; SERVO 1.2 kHz sine with vibrato, rising in chase, falling to zero on down; GROWL 70 Hz saw with noise and 9 Hz tremolo; THUD 80 ms 60 Hz sine; ARC 0.5 s gated crackle. Vent, sentry and tank use AudioStreamPlayer2D for panning. One manifest.json entry: procedural, generated by scripts/sfx_bank.gd, author Nathanial Henniges, no external source. Fallback if it sounds thin: Kenney CC0 impact/sci-fi packs, registered with URL and licence before use. Settings note 'Current chapter has no audio tracks yet' updated.

## New mechanics
- M0State: chapter_id "junction", door_blown, sentry_down, junction_cleared (+ junction_dark if the stretch ships); SAVE_VERSION 4 with ordered validation (junction => records chapter_complete; sentry_down => door_blown; cleared => sentry_down); guard methods enter_junction/blow_door/drop_sentry/clear_junction.
- PressureLine RefCounted (pure logic): arm(), advance(delta), crank(held_delta) -> turns 0..3, gauge 0..1 over 6 s, states idle/building/charged/fuse/blown/tripped, is_in_blast(x).
- SentryBrain RefCounted + Sentry CharacterBody2D using the M0Actor-style mover: tick(delta, engineer_x, in_cover, bait_x) -> patrol/chase/fixated/down; no tick while waiting_for_choice or paused.
- DropArm: 0.45 s fall tween, hit test |sentry_x - 700| <= 44, 6 s rewind, 3 s coolant-cloud Area2D on a miss.
- Hold-USE crank: accumulate Input.is_action_pressed(&"interact") only at the valve while building; turns persist on release.
- Knockdown flow _fail_beat(reason) reusing _load_game/_sync_scene with a junction branch.
- Shared foundation items: impact kit, SFX bank, hazard contact, particles (see shared_foundation).

## Feedback
- Gauge needle, hiss volume, pump pitch and vent particle amount are all functions of PressureLine.gauge; nothing animates on a fake timer.
- Each valve turn is a discrete state change: wheel 90 degrees, one light green, click, weak rumble; the seam strobes only when turn 3 is real.
- BOOM 1 disables the door shape and draws rubble in the same frame as flash, hit-stop and shake; the sentry's eyes and servo start 0.5 s later so cause and effect read in order.
- Sentry state is always visible (eye strip dim/amber/red) and audible (servo pitch), so dread comes from timing, not surprise.
- The tank's landing x is a floor mark; the cable goes slack on drop and visibly rewinds on a miss, so retry progress is physical.
- Knockdown is its own readable reaction (tilt, slide, thud, red flash) and the status line names the cause, so the reload never feels arbitrary.
- Shake and flash honour the Reduced Motion toggle; shake lives on Camera2D.offset so the existing clamp and smoothing are untouched.

## Risks
- Engine.time_scale hit-stop slows tweens and the pause overlay; one Impact node owns the restore and a headless assert checks time_scale == 1.0 after every boom and after a pause during hit-stop.
- Fuse (1.0 s, 60 px to clear) and the sentry lane (chase 230 vs walk 190, 30 px retreat from the panel) cannot be proven headless; the first knockdown must feel like the player's fault, so budget a tuning day after hands-on play; touch may need +0.2 s on the fuse.
- Three subsystems in six days is tight; cut order is Beat 5 lights-out first, then WOLF baiting (keep the refusal line), the two booms stay.
- A crushed sentry edges toward combat earlier than the roadmap's 'later moderate combat'; keep it sabotage (the building did it) and the aftermath quiet, and confirm with the user.
- SAVE_VERSION 4 touches from_dict validation and every save test; v<=3 defaults must be tested.
- Procedural boom and hiss carry the whole impact feeling; half a day of ear-testing, CC0 fallback kept open.
- Headless _walk_to has a 300-frame cap and no hazard awareness: tests walk in legs and _wait_until on PressureLine/SentryBrain state.

## Judge recommendation
Build the shared foundation first and prove it in the rooms that already exist (give the relay spark and the door opening real shake, hit-stop, flash and a procedural clank on day one), then build Option A, BLOWBACK, trimmed to two booms plus the bolt with the lights-out aftermath as the cut line. It is the most direct answer to the user's ask (loud, state-changing booms rather than more stills), it adds zero input actions so the mobile budget stays at hold-to-move plus USE, every tool is engineer-operated so the WOLF rule and the fallback rule hold by construction, and its retry reuses _load_game/_sync_scene instead of a new transient-anchor system, which is the least new code for a fail state. Option B, COOLANT GALLERY, is the right pick instead if the user wants the game to gain a permanent new verb (jump) that later rooms will reuse, and Option C, PURGE SWEEP, if dread matters more than spectacle; both are good and both cost more new systems or more tuning risk than A. Whatever is chosen, nothing has been hands-on played yet, so the plan must end with a play pass and a device run before any feel claim, per AGENT.md.

## 3. The authored-event pattern new beats should follow (m0_state.gd)

- Memory record: `record_choice()` (40-51) writes `{event_id, choice_id, selected_text, context, sequence, observed_by}`; `CHOICE_TEXT` is the only accepted text; duplicates rejected.
- Transitions are guard methods that return `bool` or a short result code string and are idempotent: `arm_breaker()` (54-58), `activate_power(actor) -> "not_ready"|"already_open"|"refused"|"cooperate"|"fallback"|"invalid_actor"` (61-75), `reach_checkpoint()`, `enter_records()`, `preserve_*()`, `complete_chapter()`. The scene (`main.gd`) only reads the code and plays feedback; it never mutates state fields directly.
- `to_dict()`/`from_dict()` validate every cross-field invariant (`door_open == (route != "")`, `cooperate ⇒ DISCLOSE`, records ⇒ checkpoint, mirror ⇒ purge). New fields: bump `SAVE_VERSION` to 4, default them for v≤3, validate under `if data["version"] >= 4`.
- Transient UI flags (`relay_refused`, `wolf_heading_to_relay`, `waiting_for_choice`) live in `main.gd` and are reset by `_new_game`/`_load_game`; they are not saved. Hazard phase, drone position, countdown remaining and "hidden" belong in this transient tier, not the save.
- WOLF's choices are deterministic rules on state (e.g. `memory.choice_id == DISCLOSE` gates cooperation) with an authored visible action (`_react_as_wolf`, `autonomous_target_x`) and a guaranteed engineer fallback (second USE = bypass, 785-822).

## 6. Risks and gotchas found while reading

- `Engine.time_scale` affects tweens, `create_timer` and physics globally; any test that awaits `create_timer(...)` after a hit-stop will stall unless the hit-stop timer uses `ignore_time_scale = true` and restores 1.0. Also restore it in `_pause_game` (UIMotion tweens process while paused).
- Audio buses are created in `GameSettings.configure()` during `_setup_settings()`; an `AudioStreamPlayer` with `bus = "Effects"` in the .tscn is instantiated before that. Assign the bus in code after configure, or add a bus layout resource.
- `_walk_to` in the tests holds the move key for up to 300 physics frames; a hazard that hurts mid-walk will make existing route tests fail unless the hazard is disarmed until the test reaches that beat, or the helper waits for a safe phase. Treat that failure as the proof the hazard is real, then fix the helper.
- `from_dict` rejects inconsistent states; a snapshot taken in the middle of a transition (e.g. `wolf_heading_to_relay` true but `door_open` false) is still valid because that flag is transient, but take snapshots only at beat boundaries to keep retries readable.
- Anything that moves the engineer off y=410 (jump, knockback) must still save inside y ≤ 414 (human) / 427 (wolf) at stations; saves happen only at stations, so this is safe as long as `_capture_positions` is not called mid-air.
- Rooms wider than 960 would require changing `_draw` rects, the actor clamp (m0_actor.gd:24,36), save validation (m0_state.gd:298) and the camera clamp (main.gd:176) together; keep rooms 960 wide.
- Public docs (`apps/docs/content/docs/*`, changelog) and `AGENT.md` require a changelog/guide update with any player-facing beat; do not claim feel validation until the route is played in a normal window.

## 3. Feedback catalogue (Godot 4.7, GL Compatibility), with starting values

**Camera shake (trauma model).** Drive `intro_camera.offset`, never `position`, so clamping and smoothing stay untouched (docs: `offset` is "useful for looking around or camera shake animations" and can bypass limits, which is fine for ±8 px). Keep a `trauma: float` in 0..1, `shake = trauma * trauma`, offset = `max_offset * shake * Vector2(noise.get_noise_2d(1.0, t * 30.0), noise.get_noise_2d(2.0, t * 30.0))` using a `FastNoiseLite` (output ≈ [-1, 1]), decay `trauma = maxf(trauma - delta / 0.6, 0.0)`, and set `offset = Vector2.ZERO` when trauma hits 0. Starting values: `max_offset` 6 px (range 4-8 at zoom 1.18), trauma add 0.35 spark, 0.5 door/seal, 0.6 scald, 0.9-1.0 boom, decay 0.6 s, noise rate 25-35 Hz. Skip rotation shake: painterly backgrounds and labels smear. Reset trauma in `_sync_scene()`, `_new_game()`, `_load_game()`, `_return_to_title()`.

**Hit-stop.** `Engine.time_scale` scales `_process`/`_physics_process` delta, Timers, SceneTreeTimers and Tweens, and must stay above 0; it does **not** affect audio. Pattern: `Engine.time_scale = 0.05`, then `get_tree().create_timer(0.07, true, false, true)` (the 4th arg is `ignore_time_scale`; 2nd keeps it running while paused) → on timeout `Engine.time_scale = 1.0`. **Mobile haptics.** `Input.vibrate_handheld(duration_ms = 500, amplitude = -1.0)`; Android needs the `VIBRATE` permission in the export preset; amplitude needs iOS 13+; web ignores amplitude and Safari/Firefox-Android do not support it; device "do not disturb"/settings can suppress it. Starting values: click 25 ms / 0.3; spark 60 / 0.5; scald 90 / 0.8; boom 160 / 1.0. Gate on `touch_enabled`.

Add one "Vibration" toggle to Settings later (both rumble and haptics) and keep every effect readable with it off; the game's "basic controls always reliable" rule applies to feedback too.

## 4. Sound

**Players and buses.** `AudioStreamPlayer` is non-positional (UI, beeps, booms, music); `AudioStreamPlayer2D` attenuates and pans by screen position relative to the listener (default: screen centre), with `max_distance` (2000 default), `attenuation`, `panning_strength` (project base × 1.0), `max_polyphony`. For this game: booms, beeps, clicks and the scald on a pool of 3-4 `AudioStreamPlayer`s (`max_polyphony` up to 4 if one node) on bus `Effects`; vent hiss and relay spark on `AudioStreamPlayer2D` (max_distance ≈ 900, attenuation 1.5) so they sit left/right as the camera pans; music later on `Music`; any future WOLF TTS on `Voice`. The runtime buses already exist with sliders. Add a hard limiter to Master via `AudioServer.add_bus_effect(0, AudioEffectLimiter.new())` (docs recommend a limiter last on Master to prevent clipping; stacked booms will clip without it). A muffled "stunned" moment after a scald = `AudioEffectLowPassFilter` on `Effects` (`cutoff_hz` default 2000, range 1-20500; use ~600 for 0.4 s) toggled with `set_bus_effect_enabled`. Headless CI uses the dummy audio driver; `play()` is safe to call.

**Procedural SFX, recommended approach: bake, don't stream.** `AudioStreamGenerator` is a real-time stream with `buffer_length` 0.5 s default (latency) and the docs warn it is CPU-heavy from GDScript (suggest 11025-22050 Hz). For one-shot SFX it is better to synthesise once at `_ready` into an `AudioStreamWAV` (`format = FORMAT_16_BITS`, `mix_rate` 22050 or 44100, `stereo = false`, `data` = little-endian signed 16-bit PCM) with a seeded `RandomNumberGenerator`, then play it normally: zero trigger latency, deterministic, no third-party asset, and the buffer is unit-testable. Keep the generator pattern (`get_stream_playback()`, `get_frames_available()`, `push_frame(Vector2)`, 22050 Hz in the official demo) only for a continuous hiss if you want it live; a 1 s looped baked clip (`loop_mode = LOOP_FORWARD`) is simpler. Use `AudioStreamRandomizer` (`random_pitch` 1.08, `random_volume_offset_db` 2) around footsteps/clicks so repeats do not sound mechanical. `save_to_wav("user://...")` lets you audition and archive clips for the register.

Packing and filters (load-bearing formulas):
```gdscript
# 16-bit mono packing
var bytes := PackedByteArray(); bytes.resize(frames * 2)
bytes.encode_s16(i * 2, int(clampf(sample, -1.0, 1.0) * 32767.0))
# one-pole low-pass per sample (dt = 1.0 / mix_rate); high-pass = x - low
var alpha := dt / (1.0 / (TAU * cutoff_hz) + dt); low += alpha * (x - low)
```
Recipes (amplitudes before the Master limiter; all with a 3-8 ms linear attack to avoid clicks):
- **Beep / terminal confirm** (breaker arm, trace copied, valve click): sine 880 Hz, or a two-step 660→880 Hz, 90-120 ms, exponential decay tau 40 ms, amplitude 0.35. Square-ish variant (`sign(sin)` mixed 30 %) reads as "terminal".
- **Spark crackle** (relay, conduit): 250-350 ms of white noise multiplied by 4-7 random gate windows of 6-18 ms, high-passed (x minus a 1.2 kHz low-pass), plus a 60 ms sine "zap" sweeping 2400→500 Hz; amplitude 0.5, decay tau 120 ms.
- **Boom** (pipe rupture, hatch slam): white noise through a low-pass whose cutoff sweeps 500→70 Hz over 600 ms, summed with a sine sub sweeping 75→35 Hz over 250 ms, overall exponential decay tau 0.35 s, 1.0-1.2 s long, soft-clip with `tanh(1.8 * s)` for weight, peak 0.9. Boom 2 = same with tau 0.5 s and a second noise layer 80 ms later.
- **Scald / vent hit**: boom-lite (tau 0.15, 0.4 s) plus a crackle layer; amplitude 0.7.
- **Hiss loop**: 1 s of noise low-passed at 3 kHz with slow 2 Hz amplitude wobble, loop, amplitude 0.25; `pitch_scale` 1.15 in beat 3.
- **Footsteps**: 50-70 ms noise burst low-passed at 600 Hz (concrete) with tau 20 ms, amplitude 0.3; trigger every 46-50 px of travel while `is_on_floor()` and `absf(velocity.x) > 1` (≈4 steps/s at 190 px/s), through a randomizer. Give WOLF a lower, two-tap variant (paws).

## 2. What the referenced games actually do (cited)

### Inside / Limbo (Playdead): trial and death, teach safe then kill
- Limbo's team called its loop "trial and death": puzzles assume the player will die first, and the gruesome death animations exist to steer players away from unworkable solutions. Lead designer Jeppe Carlsen's GDC Europe 2010 example puzzle has only three elements (switch panel, electrified floor, chain); the goal is immediately obvious and the challenge is timing and ordering. Their process: "first assume the player is their own worst enemy," then scale back as if designing for a friend. Checkpoints are frequent so deaths are learning moments, not penalties. (Wikipedia, Limbo development section; the GameSpot GDC write-up and a Playdead ex-staff interview were in search results but returned 403 to fetch.)
- Inside's first eight minutes teach every core verb with no text: the hazard appears in a safe form first (a wall you drag a box to climb) and only later under pressure (the dog chase, where "you have to move, and now!"). Cues are environmental (a fridge that "stands out via its placement and contrasting color"; a cough sound for oxygen). The write-up's pacing rule: "If you pressure them too often you risk losing them, and same if you have too many slow areas." (Game Developer, "Inside - Teaching through Level Design".)

### Little Nightmares (Tarsier): hide, wait, timing, known threat
- The fear lives in "the moment right before being caught, when the tension is still building"; "the whereabouts of the monsters are almost always known", so dread comes from waiting inches from a visible threat, not from surprise. Scale (levers too high, drawers as stairs) sells vulnerability. Sound "magnifies the atmosphere". (Hypercritic review.)
- Tarsier on companions for solo play: the AI companion must neither make players feel "led through the game like a rollercoaster" nor become "an irritating deadweight". Monster ideas are "connected by the loosest of threads" so they stay suggestive. Difficulty target: "challenge but not to the point of frustration". (Cubed3 Tarsier interview. The Unreal Engine LN2 interview was in search results but 403 on fetch.)

### Oddworld: New 'n' Tasty: stealth, possession, bounded retry
- Abe cannot attack; progress is wit (switches), stealth (shadows) and confrontation by possessing a Slig after a ~5 s chant. Possessed enemies can shoot and use switches but cannot jump, so the borrowed body is a different verb set, not a power-up. Companions "will literally keep on running into hazards or enemies unless you specifically tell them to 'wait'". (Destructoid review.)
- The remake keeps QuikSave and adds denser checkpoints so "the most any player would have to redo is a maximum length of three screens"; difficulty modes change Abe's health and enemy reaction times. (Wikipedia, New 'n' Tasty.) That is the closest published number to "retry without losing more than the current beat".

### Trine (Frozenbyte): physics as a gate, not decoration
- "most puzzles and situations can be solved with any character, or any two characters"; physics objects are not eye candy because you cannot advance without them; the safety net is "a checkpoint system that resurrects all characters, respawn points when you die". Difficulty came from "playtesting the game a lot! It's a very iterative and uncontrolled process". (Game Developer interview.)

### The Last Guardian (Ueda): companion agency and protection beats
- Boy and Trico "can only communicate via gestures, motion, and reactions"; the opening is removing spears from Trico, bond first through a caring action, not exposition; "The boy doesn't possess combat skills, so you need to work together with Trico to overcome these threats." Combat is the proof of partnership, and the relationship has "dramatic peaks and troughs". (PlayStation Blog; already in AGENT.md references.)

### Brothers (Fares): the player's hands carry the relationship
- "The whole foundation of the idea is that you connect with your left hand to the bigger brother and right hand to the little brother." The two have different abilities; the ending's beat is mechanical (pressing the dead brother's button to find strength). Fares kept introducing new mechanics ("Why not?") so no interaction became routine; co-op would have broken the metaphor ("rather cut my hands"). (Game Informer Afterwords; Shacknews.)

### Dead Cells / Katana ZERO / Hollow Knight: how hits feel good
- Motion Twin: "it's mostly because of the satisfaction of the controls... we put a lot of effort into the feedback". The GDC 2019 talk "Dead Cells: What the F*n!?" is about controls, player feedback, particles and "making players' lives easier" while staying hard; the control tricks it covers are coyote time and input buffering. (Shacknews; GDC news; Class Central listing.)
- Katana ZERO (Justin Stander): "You have to make the engine flawless. No bugs"; controls must be "an extension of the player"; the goal is a game where "you always feel like it's your fault not the game's fault so you want to try one more time"; music slows with slow motion. Each slice is sound + brief screen flash + subtle shake, and the game exposes hit-pause percentage and shake intensity as settings. (GameRevolution interview; Wayline weapon-feel article; search snippet on settings.)
- Hollow Knight: no first-party Team Cherry quote on hit freeze was fetchable (the cohorted interview returned 403). What is documented: millisecond-level input registration, readable enemy silhouettes, the Soul risk/heal trade-off, and audio EQ that changes with surface/biome (InGameNews). Player thread: shake fires as "an indicator that something is happening (usually dangerous)", caused headaches for some, and had no toggle, which players resented. Treat that as the accessibility warning.

### Game feel numbers (Vlambeer, Sakurai, SF2, Godot recipe)
- Nuclear Throne pistol, per Jan Willem Nijman (30 fps, 320x240): sound on frame 0; camera kicks back 6 px from the aim point; +4 screenshake that "degenerates quickly"; weapon sprite kick 2 px then snaps back; first bullet frame is a circle (fake muzzle flash); on impact: hit sound + material sound + character sound, enemy pushed 3 px/frame, hit animation starts with a white frame then two "ouch" frames, and a 10-20 ms screen freeze. Framing: game feel is "the tactility of a feedback loop that just works", a craft, not magic. (Infovore, "Making Game Feel".)
- Sakurai on hitstop: duration scales with damage with a hard cap; some attacks get extra (Marth's tip); both attacker and victim vibrate, horizontally when grounded and vertically in the air; vibration starts large and decays; the attacker keeps moving imperceptibly during the freeze; vibration amplitude scales with camera distance. (Source Gaming translation of Famitsu column.) Street Fighter II froze both characters for about 10 frames on hit; without hitstop "it's harder to tell a hit occurred". (Critpoints.)
- "Juice it or lose it" defines juice as "maximum output for minimum input": tween, squash/stretch, shake, particles, sound layered on an unchanged simulation. (Game Developer video post.)
- Godot shake recipe: a `trauma` 0..1 that decays (`decay = 0.8`/s), `amount = pow(trauma, 2 or 3)`, `max_offset = Vector2(100, 75)`, `max_roll = 0.1` rad, sampled from noise rather than random, applied to `Camera2D.offset` and `rotation`. (KidsCanCode Godot 4 recipe.) Eiserloh's GDC 2016 camera talk: use both translational and rotational shake in 2D. Godot docs: `offset` is "useful for looking around or camera shake animations" and may exceed limits.
- Godot audio: `AudioStreamGenerator` + `push_frame()` works from GDScript but the docs warn it is "best used from C# or from a compiled language" and to drop mix_rate to 11025 or 22050 Hz in GDScript. Implication for this project: generate short PCM clips once at startup into `AudioStreamWAV` (code-only provenance, logged in the manifest) rather than streaming in `_process`.

### Horror pacing
- Silent Hill f (Al Yang, GDC): players who say they want to be scared "really mean is they want to feel tense"; tension is anticipation and build-up; jump scares numb; hiding concrete numbers matters because "a large part of the tension of horror games relies on giving the player incomplete information"; a deliberate "system breaker" release lets players shatter tension on their own terms so they are not exhausted. (GSMGO summary of the talk.)
- Frictional (Thomas Grip): ease players in from the familiar, slow build so a "single (or just few) peaks of terror" land; combat makes players "miss many of the subtle cues"; "players might be safe, they can never be sure"; horror gets personal when it is about its effect on other characters. (Shacknews; Game Developer IGF interview.)
- Left 4 Dead's Director: "things would slow down, things would get quiet, and then things would go batshit crazy"; "You can't appreciate the action unless you have some inaction." (Shacknews, Evolve/L4D.)

## 3. Distilled rules (14)

1. **Show the hazard safe before it is lethal.** Introduce the vent, shutter or pursuer once where it cannot hurt (Inside's box wall; Limbo's three-element puzzle where the goal is obvious and only timing is unknown). The player's first death should be on a thing they already recognised.
2. **A readable chase has one input, a visible threat and a visible goal.** Hold-right (or hold-left) is the whole verb; the pursuer or hazard front is always on screen or audible; the exit is marked by contrast (color/light/label, like Inside's fridge). Speed ratio creates the "move, now" without instructions. Never add a second simultaneous input on mobile.
3. **Death costs one beat, never more.** Oddworld's remake bound redo to about three screens; Limbo checkpoints before every puzzle. Here: fail resets to the last safe point or the start of the current beat with no save write and no story state loss.
4. **Make the death itself informative.** Limbo's gruesome deaths steer players from wrong answers. The fail animation should point at the cause (the vent that caught you, the shutter edge), then cut to retry within a second.
5. **Failure must read as the player's fault.** Katana ZERO: flawless engine, controls as extension; Dead Cells: coyote time and input buffering. For a game with no jump, the equivalent is a generous USE window, no input eaten by UI, and hazards whose collision matches their art.
6. **Alternate pressure and quiet.** Inside, L4D and Frictional all say the same thing: contrast is the pacing tool; aim for one real peak per room and a quiet beat with WOLF after it (AGENT.md already asks for this).
7. **Tension is anticipation plus incomplete information.** Known threat position (Little Nightmares), unknown timing; no visible counters for the dangerous thing (Silent Hill f). A countdown you can hear but not read beats a timer bar.
8. **Give the player a release valve they control.** Silent Hill f's "system breaker". In this game the valve can be the engineer's own act (slamming a breaker, sealing a door) so the "boom" is caused, not watched.
9. **A companion's intervention is earned by a prior caring or shared action, and shown in motion.** Last Guardian: bond first, gestures not words, cooperation proven in danger. The existing `_react_as_wolf` lean (main.gd:958-970) is the right instinct; the intervention beat needs WOLF to physically do something that changes state (block, pull, hold) rather than a line.
10. **The companion must neither lead nor be deadweight, and must stay optional.** Tarsier's rollercoaster/deadweight warning plus Trine's "any character can solve it": every protection beat needs the engineer-only route (a slower, scarier manual path), and WOLF's help is a choice he makes, not a prompt the player presses.
11. **Impact is a fixed stack fired by one state change, in this order:** sound (frame 0) → white/flash frame on the thing hit → hit-stop 10-20 ms (2-10 frames; scale with severity, cap it) → camera kick toward/away plus shake that decays fast → knockback/displacement → particles/debris → secondary material/character sounds. All of it reads from the real state flag so there is never fake feedback.
12. **Shake is trauma-driven, noise-sampled, applied to `Camera2D.offset`, and capped.** `shake = trauma^2`, decay ~0.8/s, small amplitudes at 960x540 (think 4-8 px, not 100), translational plus a little roll, scaled by zoom; expose a reduce/disable toggle (Katana ZERO does; Hollow Knight's lack of one drew complaints).
13. **Hit-stop vibrates both parties, decays, and keeps a sliver of motion.** Sakurai: horizontal vibration on ground, vertical in air, large-to-small, attacker never fully frozen. In Godot: freeze via `Engine.time_scale` or per-node `process_mode` for a few frames while a tiny sprite jitter continues.
14. **Sound carries the feel; layer three sounds per hit and let music react.** Vlambeer's action + material + character layering; Katana ZERO slows the music in slow-mo; Hollow Knight EQs by surface. With no audio yet, procedurally generated clips (noise bursts, sine thumps, filtered clicks) baked once into `AudioStreamWAV` are enough to prove the stack and carry clean provenance.
