### F1. Ellie (The Last of Us) — Dyckhoff's own chapter [COMPANION-PLAY-1], GDC Vault abstract [COMPANION-PLAY-2]
Primary: Max Dyckhoff (Naughty Dog), "Ellie: Buddy AI in The Last of Us", Game AI Pro 2, ch. 35, pp. 431-442 (CRC Press 2015; free PDF on gameaipro.com). Read in full (text extracted locally, not copied into repo).
- Goal stated up front: avoid "a tedious 'escort' quest, generally getting underfoot, or turning into mindless drones with no agency"; "Ellie never annoyed or frustrated the player".
- Rule 1, stay close: a close buddy has fewer problems with enemies; "if she is far away from the player, she is easily forgotten". If she is placed like the player and still seen, the player blames themselves, not her.
- Follow positions: a torus ("follow region") around the leader; candidates from 3 sets of navmesh rays: (a) leader->region clear path, (b) forward from candidate so she does not face a wall, (c) player->forward point so no obstacle gets between them (without it she stood on the other side of a doorway or fence). Rated EVERY FRAME on: distance to leader, staying on the same side of the leader, hiding/exposing to targets, NOT IN FRONT OF THE PLAYER, distance to other buddies; a smoothing term reduces noise.
- Movement filters: short moves only when necessary; never run past the leader unless otherwise stopping somewhere bad; "close enough" arrival so she does not crowd the player.
- Speed by distance: walk short, run mid, sprint far; one fixed speed per mode; to stop walk/run oscillation she scales animation speed by up to 25%.
- Dodging: she does not pre-emptively move out of the player's way; only at the last second a canned dodge plus a line "admonishing the player for encroaching on her personal space". Turned an annoyance into character.
- Teleport: "never teleport the buddies for the purpose of keeping them near the player" (audio would hear footsteps jump; she'd swap sides). Only exception: rushing in to break a melee grapple, while camera control is locked and pointed away.
- Grapples: originally the player had to save her every time -> "tedious", escort quest. Fix: she escapes herself and is untargetable for 15-30 s. Player-saves-Ellie kept only "intentionally and infrequently".
- Utility on long timers: brick throw "on a really long timer"; gifting tuned after a stuck playtester got ammo "every minute" (devalued it). "If Ellie saved you from a melee grapple just once during the game, you would remember that moment fondly".
- Visibility: own stealth AI worked "about 90%-95% of the time"; they chose to make buddies invisible to enemies while the player is stealthy: "if they ruin what the player is trying to do just once, it's all in vain."
- Shooting: default is NOT to shoot; permission only when the player is loud or in immediate danger. Off-screen hits do no damage; she lands hits when the player has not seen her shoot recently or is in danger. "furtive" idle fills gaps.
- Callouts: "whenever a buddy calls out an enemy, the enemy is there for the player to see"; bad callouts "reflected badly on Ellie's intelligence".
- Ambience: idles (cleaning knife, shoelace, hair) and an "explore" system of designer-placed points of interest; "only took a day or so to implement".
- Team: one core engineer, 5 months.
- Note: Kill Screen (2014) relays a "within a meter" dialogue rule; it is not in the chapter, so UNVERIFIED.
WHAT IT MEANS FOR WOLF: 2D side view makes the torus a 1D band: WOLF's slot = behind the engineer (already in code: side away from facing), never in front of them in the direction of travel, never through a doorway/across a sealed door from them (already: follow_max_x clamp). Add: arrival tolerance so he does not micro-shuffle; scale his walk/trot animation +-25% instead of flipping gait at a threshold; no teleport while on camera; any WOLF help (bait, crank, warn) on a long cooldown and authored, not every attempt; WOLF must never be the thing that alerts the sentry; any warning (growl, ear flick) only when the threat is on screen or audibly real.

### F2. Elizabeth (BioShock Infinite) — Abercrombie GDC 2014 [COMPANION-PLAY-3 vault abstract], reports [COMPANION-PLAY-4 Game Informer, COMPANION-PLAY-5 The Escapist, COMPANION-PLAY-6 GamesBeat], Levine interviews [COMPANION-PLAY-7 GamingBolt relaying RPS 2011, COMPANION-PLAY-8 Georgia Straight 2013]
Talk itself is members-only on GDC Vault; the following are press reports of the talk (secondary but from the session).
- Goal side: borrowed from football, "the goalie should always be between the player and the goal" (Escapist); Elizabeth is "often 'goal side' to the player, staying just ahead of them" (Game Informer, Kimberley Wallace, 2014-03-18). The reason (Game Informer): if she only followed behind there would be no direct interaction, because players "would have to stop, turn around, and look at her". [FACT-CHECK: the earlier wording said SHE must stop, turn and look at the player; the article says the reverse: a trailing companion forces the PLAYER to turn round to see her.]
- Look-at markers with "levels of interest, time of interaction, and distance to the object"; "different timers and cool-downs" so she examines things naturally (Escapist; Game Informer).
- Off-screen finds cued by sound: she would "sigh" or say "hmm" (Game Informer).
- Teleport: "quietly teleport Elizabeth to the player's side if they happened to get too far ahead" (i.e. when the player got too far ahead of her), to avoid pathing around obstacles such as railings (GamesBeat report, 2014-03-19). Goal-side only arrived in the final year of a ~4.5-year development (GamesBeat).
- Callouts: players could not find the sniper she pointed at, so an "icon spotlight" was added "much like a dramatic entrance would require a spotlight in the theater" (GamesBeat). Conclusion slide: "Be player-facing", "Think about theater" (Escapist).
- Levine: "a very you-centered AI. You are not obligated to take care of her in the way that you are in an escort mission"; she can "toss you ammo, point out enemies" (RPS 2011 via GamingBolt). "She's not somebody who's along for the ride or somebody that you have to protect" (Georgia Straight, Blaine Kyllo, 2013-03-26).
DISAGREEMENT INSIDE THE CORPUS: Naughty Dog refused teleport-to-keep-up on principle (audio/side swaps), Irrational used it off-screen. For a 2D side view where the whole room width is often on screen, WOLF has fewer unseen moments, so Dyckhoff's rule fits better: no visible teleport; off-camera catch-up only by running fast through a door or edge, with footstep audio continuous.
WHAT IT MEANS FOR WOLF: goal-side suits WOLF's scouting/leading beats (Lead state), but ONLY when the goal is known and the player asked or the beat is authored; otherwise follow (see Alyx, below). A look-at marker list per room (interest, dwell time, max distance, cooldown) is the cheap way to make WOLF read as curious. Any WOLF "sniff/point" must highlight something the player can then see.

### F3. Alyx and squads (Half-Life 2, Episode One, Episode Two) — Valve developer commentary transcribed on Combine OverWiki [COMPANION-PLAY-9 EP1, COMPANION-PLAY-10 EP2, COMPANION-PLAY-11 HL2 20th anniversary commentary, 2024]
- Nagging (Matt T. Wood, EP1 "alyxnag"): "Hurry up!" lines; "after about three minutes of this, the one thing it definitely did was make the player hate Alyx." Led Valve to "switch Alyx from generally leading players to almost always following"; players "especially disliked virtually any hint of bossiness".
- Pace (Bill Van Buren, "alyxcitadeltone"): urgency through her acting was "unsustainable"; if the player did not advance "Alyx eventually ran out of lines or animations". Rule adopted: she "is always content to proceed at whatever pace the player chooses"; impatient lines were cut.
- Speed (Doug Wood, "alyxrunspeed"): following "looks and feels the best when she's moving faster than the player's normal speed, but slower than the player's sprint speed." Took "many playtests".
- Hints (Chet Faliszek, "alyxthekey"): better when "Alyx stated a goal and then stepped aside"; "almost all of Alyx's hint lines were cut"; but players "did want to be able to explicitly request help" -> hints only on request (+use on Alyx).
- Don't steal the fun (Marc Laidlaw, EP2 003): she is "careful not to steal the player's fun by doing it all herself."
- Injury pacing (EP2-Comment090, speaker shown as "Q!" on the wiki): playtesters "did not enjoy waiting for her to catch up, or going back to retrieve her"; Valve started her recovery in the preceding map so she could "run at regular speed" here.
- Turret feel (Richard Lord, EP2 150): firing at everything "made her feel a bit like a turret"; slowed and aimed fire.
- Presentation (Robin Walker, "crouchingalyx"): "Character AI is all about putting on a good show"; crouch-and-aim made her "a whole lot more fun to watch" without changing effectiveness. ("alyx_ledge"): "ultimately what matters most is how it looks to the player."
- Out of the way (Matt Scott, "justhavetodoit"): she climbs out to a place the player can't reach so she is "out of the way of interesting player challenges"; called it a "crutch".
- Praise as reward (Doug Wood, "youremynewhero"): "positive feedback from Alyx is one of our primary means of rewarding players" — someone "witnessed and appreciated their heroics".
- Separation (Ido Magal, "separatefromalyx"): separated for the strider fight because "boss battles are the player's big hero moments".
- HL2 (2024 commentary): without boundaries "combat AI could drift significantly away from the player", which led to the citizen player squad (Ken Birdwell, cn_256_citizen_squads); "Off-camera, the squad would 'cheat' in various ways—regenerating health between fights, moving quickly to catch up, and even teleporting when necessary" (Matt Wright, cn_255_citizen_squad_details; this is Valve's commentary wording, not a transcriber paraphrase). Dog in combat: "it almost felt like we'd be building a new game inside of the game" [exact wording, fact-check 2] so it became a choreographed scene (Eric Kirchmer, cn_245). Alyx's multi-tool used as a key "a bit too much" (Laura Dubuk, cn_200).
WHAT IT MEANS FOR WOLF: (1) WOLF follows by default; leads only in authored beats. (2) WOLF's follow speed must sit between the engineer's walk and jog: walk 80 < WOLF < jog 190 for his settled trot, with a catch-up gait above jog for when he falls behind. Today FOLLOW_SPEED 220 > WALK 190 (the single current speed); once walk 80 / jog 190 ship, WOLF needs two speeds (e.g. ~120 trot, ~230 catch-up) — UNVERIFIED tuning values, set by play. (3) No "hurry" lines ever, even during the Blowback; urgency comes from the hazard, not WOLF. (4) WOLF never solves the puzzle for the engineer; he states/shows the goal and steps aside. (5) Hints on request only — and the engineer's existing USE on WOLF is the natural request input. (6) WOLF reacting to the engineer's success (one short pleased sound/pose after the tank drop) is a reward; keep it.

### F4. Trico (The Last Guardian, genDESIGN/SIE Japan Studio, 2016) [COMPANION-PLAY-12 Famitsu CEDEC 2017, COMPANION-PLAY-13 PSU relaying The Guardian 2016, COMPANION-PLAY-14 Gigazine 2017, COMPANION-PLAY-15 Wikipedia (reception citations), COMPANION-PLAY-16 Game Informer 2010]
- Masanobu Tanaka (animation director / lead game designer, genDESIGN), CEDEC 2017 "トリコの動かし方", session held 2017-08-31, reported by Famitsu (writer イズミロボ・ササ / Izumi Robo Sasa) 2017-09-01; Tanaka is credited there as animation director and lead game designer on Trico: objects carry interest levels that change; Trico goes to the highest, interest drops as a desire is satisfied, which reads as will. Look-at is "獅子舞方式" (lion-dance method): the head is fixed on the target first and the body turns beneath it; the rotation pivot is near the nose tip; head avoids clipping walls (confirmed in the article in fact-check 2: without it the head pushed into walls or a pillar blocked the gaze). Action chosen by space: room = eat; cramped = paw at it; impossible = vocalise a request to the boy. Footfalls placed after lift-off against terrain/obstacles. Stated aim: "AIの目的は、トリコが心を持っているように、ユーザーに感じてもらうこと" (the AI's purpose is to make users feel Trico has a heart).
- Ueda (The Guardian interview 2016, relayed by PSU, Michael Harradence, 2016-07-05; original not opened): "If you can fully control a character, what's the point? It becomes a pet. But at the opposite end if you can't control it at all it becomes a nuisance, a barrier to progression." (This resolves the earlier dossier's "quote as relayed": the wording is confirmed by a second outlet, still not the original page.)
- Ueda (Gigazine 2017-01-15): realism is "an outcome of removing unnatural parts over and over again. There are no shortcuts"; "If there were no motions of muscles, or reactions when it jumps and lands on the ground, you can't feel its weightiness."
- Reception (Wikipedia citing IGN, Marty Sliva 2016-12-05; Game Revolution 2016-12-05): Trico's puzzle behaviour plus camera made sections "more frustrating"; Trico sometimes did tasks without input, making commands feel "ineffective and unnecessary".
- 9 years in development (2007-2016).
WHAT IT MEANS FOR WOLF: Copy the cheap, readable parts: interest targets with decay; lion-dance look-at (head locks to target, body follows over ~0.2-0.4 s; UNVERIFIED timing, tune); "space decides the action" (if he can't reach, he signals to the engineer by sound + head point). Do NOT copy the latency: in a short slice with an engineer-only fallback, WOLF's hesitation must be an authored refusal beat, never input lag. A refusal must look different from "didn't hear you" (see the Hold/Refuse pose rule below).

### F5. Atreus (God of War, 2018) — Hayato Yoshidome, "Raising Atreus for Battle in God of War", GDC 2019 [COMPANION-PLAY-17 GDC Vault page, COMPANION-PLAY-18 GDC YouTube upload]
Read via the YouTube auto-captions (kept only in scratch, not in the repo); wording below is paraphrase or short caption quotes, which may have small transcription errors.
- Four core pillars (~03:00-04:00): support the player; "should never get in the way of the player"; anything he does must be helpful and never make the player feel bad; "you should not have to babysit him". Breaking any one makes it feel like "a bad escort mission". He cites Dyckhoff's Ellie talk as his starting point.
- Failed prototypes: an "initiator" mode made players sneak around (wrong play pattern) and using him was "mindless"; switchable offensive/defensive modes were ignored "in the heat of the battle"; dividing the battlefield like a multiplayer teammate failed because players never saw him.
- Three diagnosed problems: PRESENCE (people did not see where he was), PERCEIVED IMPACT (did not know what his help did), weak enemies.
- Presence fixes: bow used as a melee weapon so his action is next to the target and readable; "dynamic poses" (dialled "the anime knob by like thirty percent"), side-on silhouettes, a crouched dash pose that tells you he is going into position; positioning BEHIND THE PLAYER'S TARGET, in the player's field of view, so his movement "had an intentionality".
- Numbers: a self-imposed "three, four second rule" for Atreus to come on screen and post up next to the player's target; off-screen he teleports to the point on the camera frustum edge nearest his target; "I want to make sure Atreus was a reliable teammate not a realistic teammate"; he is more reliable off-screen than on, because on-screen he must run. Q&A: realism of instant warp did not matter: "if Atreus becomes really unreliable you kind of don't want to use Atreus".
- Timing: best moment for companion help is during the player's long-recovery moves, because "that small beat" lets them register what he did; never interrupt the player's aim or knock their target out of their combo ("respect the reaction hierarchy"; "predict the player's intent").
- Reliability of the button: pressing the son button must always produce the action; all his actions are interruptible; his update loop "can't be round robin because all the actions will be delayed". When he can't (out of arrows) he says so out loud.
- Death: he cannot die; leaving him incapacitated is the player's choice. "We didn't want to babysit".
- Damage: never surpasses Kratos'; he creates shortcuts (example kill ~14 s without his help, much shorter with), not the spotlight.
- Arc: early-game he is weaker by design; late-game he disobeys the button for story — "it feels like a really bad companion... but narratively it all made sense"; players were proud when he showed learned skills in the final fight.
WHAT IT MEANS FOR WOLF: his four pillars are exactly the WOLF brief. Concrete transfers: (1) any USE on WOLF / any request must produce a visible WOLF response on the next frame or two (ear/head turn), even if the answer is no; (2) his helpful act lands in a pause the player created (after the crank finishes, after the tank hits), not mid-input; (3) WOLF's help must be visible next to its effect (put WOLF at the valve/sentry he affects, in frame); (4) WOLF cannot die or need rescue in this slice (already decided: "never rescues", and Secure the Evidence has no WOLF fail state); (5) the late-game "he no longer obeys" beat is a known risk that Santa Monica accepted for story — WOLF refusals should be story beats with an engineer-only path, as decided.

### F6. Yorda (ICO, Team Ico 2001) [COMPANION-PLAY-19 shmuplations 2002 Ueda/Kaido interview translation, COMPANION-PLAY-20 OPS2 UK Feb 2002 transcription, COMPANION-PLAY-21 shmuplations CONTINUE 2005 Ueda interview, COMPANION-PLAY-22 Jurie Horneman GDC 2004 notes]
- Ueda (2002): "If Yorda's movements didn't look like they were actually thoughtful, guiding her around would be an annoying chore for the player." The hand-hold was invented for "more direct communication between the characters in-game".
- Ueda (OPS2 UK, Feb 2002): "outside of the gameplay Yorda often makes strange movements: she strolls around, follows birds and points to things"; this makes the pair feel they are "sharing an adventure, co-operating together".
- Ueda (CONTINUE 2005): people asked "why don't you add an icon above the girl's head, so the player can know how she's feeling?"; ICO is "a game with no tutorial, no gauges"; he later doubted himself ("maybe there should have been a life gauge?").
- GDC 2004 (Horneman's attendee notes): Ueda and Kaido "ruthlessly removed everything from the game which did not support the emotional experience"; Ueda had fingertips re-animated late.
WHAT IT MEANS FOR WOLF: idle business (sniffing a vent, tracking a drone light, pointing his head at the next door) is what makes following feel like company; WOLF's mood stays diegetic (pose/eye/sound), no mood icon. But Ico's companion-as-fail-state (Yorda taken = game over) is the thing NOT to copy; the earlier reference already says this.

### F10. Valve's shipped follow and speech numbers — Source SDK 2013 code (Valve, public GitHub) [COMPANION-PLAY-29 ai_behavior_follow.cpp, COMPANION-PLAY-30 ai_playerally.cpp/.h, COMPANION-PLAY-31 npc_dog.cpp]
Primary: the code Valve published for Half-Life 2 / Episodes NPCs (units are Hammer units; HL2's player is 72 units tall, so 1 unit ~ 1 inch).
FOLLOW FORMATIONS (ai_behavior_follow.cpp ~lines 2196-2460):
- "Simple" (default): slot range 96-120 units from the leader; slot tolerance 128; leader must move 36 units before the formation resets (targetMoveTolerance); repath if the goal moves 60; WALK (not run) when within 190 units (walkTolerance); may leave formation for cover/LOS/chase within 300.
- "Tight": 0 range, tolerance 48, reset on 6 units, walk within 12.
- "Sidekick": 120-160 units, height range 256, flag AIFF_REQUIRE_LOS_OUTSIDE_COMBAT (must keep line of sight to the player outside combat), same 36/60/190/300 tolerances. Which formation each Alyx map used is set per level in ai_goal_follow, so "Alyx used Sidekick" is UNVERIFIED.
- In body heights (72): Simple ~1.3-1.7 heights centre-to-centre, walk within ~2.6 heights; Sidekick ~1.7-2.2 heights.
- Dead-band: the leader must move ~0.5 body height (36 units) before followers re-plan — this is the "don't micro-shuffle" rule in numbers (agrees with Dyckhoff's "close enough" filter).
SPEECH TIMERS (ai_playerally.cpp lines 33-127; columns = global category delay, personal category delay, per-concept delay, in seconds):
- Category defaults: IDLE speech has a GLOBAL 10-20 s gap shared by ALL allies (after any idle line, no ally makes another idle line for 10-20 s); IMPORTANT and PRIORITY have no category gap.
- TLK_HELLO is speak-once and, when spoken, sets the GLOBAL idle-category gap to 5-10 s (columns 1-2), not a per-concept repeat window. [FACT-CHECK: corrected from "per-concept 5-10 s".]
- Per-concept repeats: TLK_STARE 180 s; TLK_PLPUSH (player pushes ally) 15-30 s; TLK_PLRELOAD 60 s; TLK_HELP_ME 7-10 s; TLK_STARTCOMBAT 30 s; TLK_WATCHOUT 30-45 s; TLK_MOBBED 10-12 s; TLK_MANY_ENEMIES 45-60 s; TLK_DANGER 5-7 s (priority); TLK_PLDEAD 100 s; TLK_HIDEANDRELOAD 45-60 s; TLK_ALLY_KILLED 15-30 s. Note the code sets the concept timer with the MIN value twice (line ~254), so in practice the per-concept gap is the minimum.
- sv_npc_talker_maxdist = 1024 units: allies beyond ~14 body heights do not try to speak. This check, and the "someone else is talking" semaphore, are skipped for SPEECH_PRIORITY concepts (DANGER, PLDEAD, HIDEANDRELOAD, LOOK), which also ignore category gaps (ai_playerally.cpp ~lines 261-270 and 1444-1470). That is the shipped basis for "danger may break the idle gap".
- Alyx (npc_alyx_episodic.cpp): attack announcements on a 3-5 s timer; "heard sound" speech timers of 4 s and 10 s with a 1-3 s reaction delay.
- Dog: dog_max_wait_time 7 s (catch window when Dog throws an object).
WHAT IT MEANS FOR WOLF: concrete starting values for WOLF's sound/line budget (to tune by play): ambient WOLF vocal (huff, whine, servo chirp) at most once per 10-20 s; the same WOLF reaction to the same cue not again within its own window (e.g. "engineer bumps WOLF" 15-30 s, warning growl 5-7 s, "look at this" 30-45 s); one-shot lines (hello, first relay comment) speak once. Danger cues may break the idle gap; idle chatter never interrupts a danger cue. WOLF does not vocalise when > ~14 body heights from the engineer (off the far side of a wide room) except to answer a call.
The engineer is ~72 px tall in world space (rowan sprite 384 px x 0.188 scale; includes some transparent padding, so approximate), which by coincidence equals HL2's 72-unit player, so Valve's numbers map close to 1 unit ~ 1 world px. Valve's Simple slot (96-120 px centre-to-centre for two ~32 px-wide humans) leaves ~64-88 px of air; WOLF's FOLLOW_GAP 88 (centre to centre) with WOLF_REACH 67 + HUMAN_REACH 17 leaves ~4 px of air. That is far tighter than Valve's default; it suits a companion who presses close (Ellie's "keep buddies really close"), but in a side view WOLF's long body then sits in front of or behind the engineer's silhouette on every stop. Treat 4 px air as a deliberate choice to verify in rendered stills and play, not a default.

### F12. Response-time limits (for "acknowledge instantly") [COMPANION-PLAY-40]
- Jakob Nielsen, "Response Times: The 3 Important Limits" (Nielsen Norman Group, 1993): 0.1 s is "the limit for having the user feel that the system is reacting instantaneously"; 1.0 s is the limit for "the user's flow of thought to stay uninterrupted"; 10 s for keeping attention.
- -> WOLF: visible acknowledgement (ear/head snap, eye brighten, short servo chirp) within 0.1 s (6 frames at 60 fps) of any USE/request on him; his decision (help, wait, refuse) readable within 1.0 s. This fills the earlier reference's "No source gives seconds" gap with a general HCI source, not a game source; game confirmation comes from Atreus (button must always produce the action; no round-robin delay).

## Synthesis: rules for WOLF in play (numbers are starting values to tune in play, each with its basis)
1. FOLLOW, DON'T LEAD. Default state is Follow; Lead only in authored beats with wait points. Basis: Alyx nagging -> "make the player hate Alyx" after ~3 min; Valve switched to "almost always following" [9]; Chou wait points/hysteresis [33].
2. SLOT BEHIND, SAME ROOM, NEVER IN THE PATH. Stand on the side away from the engineer's facing (already in code), never across a doorway/seal, never on a USE spot. Basis: Ellie rating "not being in front of the player", third ray keeps her in the "same room" [1]; Valve Sidekick requires LOS outside combat [29].
3. DEAD-BAND AND ARRIVAL TOLERANCE. Don't re-target until the engineer moves ~36 px (~0.5 body height); accept "close enough". Basis: Valve targetMoveTolerance 36 of a 72-unit player [29]; Ellie "close enough" filter, short moves only when necessary [1].
4. GAIT BY DISTANCE. Within ~190 px (~2.6 body heights) match the engineer's gait (walk with walk, trot with jog); beyond that run at 1.0-1.7x the engineer's top speed (today 220/190 = 1.16x). Scale WOLF's cycle +-25% rather than flip gaits at a threshold. Basis: Valve walkTolerance 190 [29]; Alyx speed between player normal 190 and sprint 320 (ratio 1.0-1.68) [9][29 hl2_player.cpp]; Dyckhoff 25% animation scaling [1].
5. NO ON-CAMERA TELEPORT. Off-camera catch-up allowed (enter from the frame edge/door) with continuous footstep audio; arrive in frame for an authored assist within 3-4 s. Basis: Dyckhoff's refusal [1]; Irrational's off-screen teleport [6]; Atreus frustum-edge warp and "three, four second rule" [18].
6. ACKNOWLEDGE IN 0.1 s, DECIDE IN 1 s. Any input aimed at WOLF gets a visible response within 0.1 s, even a refusal; never a silent non-response. Basis: Nielsen [40]; Atreus button reliability [18]; Trico latency criticism [15].
7. NEVER NEEDS BABYSITTING. WOLF can't die, be captured or need rescue in this slice; if threatened he frees himself. Player-saves-WOLF only as a rare authored beat. Basis: Ellie grapple self-escape + 15-30 s untargetable [1]; Atreus can't die [18]; Levine "you are not obligated to take care of her" [7].
8. NEVER BREAKS THE PLAYER'S PLAN. WOLF is invisible to the sentry except in the authored bait beat. Basis: Ellie invisibility; 90-95% stealth wasn't enough [1].
9. HELP IS RARE, VISIBLE AND WELL TIMED. Long cooldowns; help lands in a pause the engineer created; WOLF is in frame next to what he affects. Basis: Ellie long timers, gifting "every minute" devalued it [1]; Atreus presence, recovery-window timing [18]; Dyckhoff "if the player doesn't see what they are doing, then it is wasted effort" [1].
10. DON'T STEAL THE FUN OR THE SPOTLIGHT. WOLF never finishes the engineer's puzzle; he states/shows the goal and steps aside; hints only on request. Basis: Laidlaw EP2 [10]; Faliszek hint-on-request [9]; Atreus never surpasses Kratos [18].
11. SPEECH/SOUND BUDGET. No hurry lines ever. Ambient WOLF vocal <= 1 per 10-20 s; per-cue windows (warning growl 5-7 s, bumped 15-30 s, "look at this" 30-45 s); speak-once lines speak once; danger may break the idle gap; callouts only for threats the player can see. Basis: Valve ally speech table [30] (shipped code uses the minimum of each per-concept range); Valve lead nag every 3 s [41] is the behaviour to avoid; Alyx nag/pace [9]; Ellie callouts [1].
12. OFF-SCREEN: SOUND + STATE MARKER. Edge marker only while out of frame, showing direction AND state (waiting / coming / busy-or-refusing); no mood icon. Basis: Lana icon [24]; Elizabeth "sigh"/"hmm" cues [4]; Ico no gauges [21].
13. IDLE BUSINESS FROM PLACED INTEREST POINTS. Per room 2-4 look-at/explore points with interest, dwell, max distance, cooldown; lion-dance look-at (head first). Basis: Elizabeth look-at markers [5]; Ellie explore system "a day or so" [1]; Trico interest decay and head-first look-at [12]; Yorda birds [20].
14. WITNESS AND MEMORY. WOLF reacts to the engineer's successes; the same idle changes after a relationship beat. Basis: Alyx "someone witnessed" [9]; Brothers flower idle [23].
15. REFUSAL LOOKS LIKE A DECISION. Distinct pose + sound + where his head points; never mistaken for lag; engineer-only route visible. Basis: Trico reception [15]; Ueda "nuisance, a barrier to progression" [13]; Atreus late disobedience "feels like a really bad companion" [18].
16. PHONE. A real share of phone players play without sound, so the off-screen marker must carry state alone. Data (fact-check 2): Appington's Amplify metrics, 2013, 73% of mobile gamers played with volume on (UK 58%, US 78%), so about 27% did not [43]; a 2022 self-report survey of 2,400 app users by TapResearch claims only 8% play with game sound on [44]; one developer's own telemetry saw only 2% switch audio off in-game [45], which does not count device mute. Magnitudes conflict and none is for this game's audience, so the share is UNVERIFIED, but the direction supports the rule; WOLF's 0.1 s acknowledgement doubles as touch feedback when a tap lands on him.
